title: "Þe Great Wen"

(A Standard Habbie, written in light of my uncle Rabbie- may God convert him.)

1\. Out in the treacle-tilth sea
Wee pink snake tiller under me:
My hoe come down and clave your body -
An evil fate.
You left no cry, no sigh, nor plea;
’Twas too late.

2\. Still, we build our ploughs yet bigger:
First man, then ox, then horse, then tractor;
This last does dice up like a blender,
Swift swords a-wheeling.
Then nothing’s left but brackish desert,
From rape unfeeling.
\-

3\. Where once the little birdies sang,
While they did pick the grains and gang
Them to their nests, where bright young pang
For some modest fare,
But now no crow, nor hare, nor nothing
That dare go there.

4\. “But we must fill our children’s needs.”
Oh, what excuse is feigned for ‘needs’!
“Besides, we are not here the fiends
Of this cruel story;
For God has made us lords and keepers,
In all our glory...”

5\. Tell me, Christian man, right now:
Did Garden-Father make a trowel,
Or was it after, for the cow,
By God created?
And will then our incestuous row
Be forever fated?!

6\. And how do you intend to feed -
Deliver plump to our children’s needs,
When this fine net of many breeds,
Of creatures broken?
Will Technique then have us freed
From words Unspoken?

7\. No, rather, I fear, at very least,
From the Mid-west to the Near East,
Our paradigm of rape and feast
Still proves us stupid.
This…fluttering on metal beast,
Breathing fumes putrid.

~ A Stay ~

8\. The mind foresees many things,
But only some of what time brings;
The unseen threads and hidden windings
Elude its model.
And all its boasts and reckonings
Are only prattle.

9\. The end result’s much the same,
As if no thought had fed the flame
Beneath the ivory dome’s own frame;
The mind unwielding,
While hidden currents rule the game
Our ends lie wielding.

10\. More brutish than those beasts of old,
That we once did shackle and hold,
And cast aside to rot and mold,
When done with them.
To man we’ll do the same when cold
Men’s hearts grow wen.

11\. Christ, terrible Judge, shall come,
See children in our mouths become,
See wives to neighbors’ keeping succumb,
And all upended!
Torrents, earthquakes, wild fires drum;
The end, upended!

12\. “Need, need” -  define need,
Rightly find the truth of need,
Not calling lusts our need -
All might be aright.
See, there! To sort this knot of need
Is virtue alight.

13\. Oft here and there she sudden springs,
As swift as stars in heaven sing,
Of glories old and endless wellings
That rectify it:
The unwound, limp, and flabby spring
Of our sad spirit.

14\. The Lord of all creation pined;
Hunger sits in bowel and mind,
Hidden in the poor behind;
Your lack.
“Above their need - children, friends, kind!”
John’s attack.
\-

15\. Poverty, she reveals the meaning
Of what is needful, what is healing
For body and soul, and every being,
In all things else.
All we must do is then be willing
To suffer loss.

~ A Return ~

16\. I see the tree grow to maturity.
First, it starts off in seed obscurity,
Then to branches and leaf-virility.
And then it stops.
Contented with its shapely iridity,
The sky it tops.
\~

17\. When cancer takes her stroll in town,
And country aside, no one can drown
Where no sea is, yet still in brown
Our rich soil coarse.
Here, though it seems a crown;
It’s just a farce.
\-

18\. The rolling hills of grains and corn,
still growing in the shape once worn,
Of farmers’ days in times forlorn -
Something’s different.
Then, worked with the land of yore,
Now, land indifferent.

19\. How long will this mistress prove
Still tolerant of wheel above,
Which once we thought she well might love
To feel atop her?
Or mind we she can nothing love -
No spirit about her?

20\. Not only scouring grass and shrub,
But taking all, not leaving trub;
Roombas carry crumbs to flub
Into their trash bins.
Not speck or morsel, nor a stub,
For false-friends.

21\. Perhaps your mother, fat on beef,
And dazed by phone, TV, and sleep,
Coffee and tea from far-off grief
Of sober slave’s hand,
Ought better never drawn one breath,
Ne’er seen; Ne’er-Land.

22\. Damn you, crazy, dumb, and lazy man,
For inciting a fuming diatribe,
While all I want is peace of mind,
Working my garden green and mild,
As if a playing child.
Have I ever sinned or not done wrong?
No, not yet; ever.

23\. And so, dear worm, I am sorry true
That your rose flesh should be turned blue
On account of my and my kind’s untrue
Hearts towards your kind.
Turn in the soul and there renew
That peace we find.

~ Epilogue ~

24\. How could I, Lady of clemency,
From these thoughts - veiled piety,
And list away to heaven already,
Beneath thy wing?
Yet not the time; the work is not
Finished.
